About Us

Built from community need, guided by service.

Founded in August 2020 in Freetown, Sierra Leone, the Moandemah Foundation was created in response to the everyday barriers that keep too many children, youth, and women from reaching their potential. Our mission is simple and urgent: to empower communities through education, health, and sustainable livelihoods.

We support children who lack school supplies, girls who miss class because they do not have hygiene materials, and families living in communities where schools need renovation, furniture, and better learning environments. Every intervention is designed to restore hope and make practical progress visible in the lives of people we serve.

Our Programs

Focused support where the need is immediate.

Hygiene Materials for Teenage Girls

We provide dignity kits and menstrual health support so girls can stay in school with confidence and consistency.

Back-to-School Supplies Drive

Exercise books, bags, pens, and uniforms help children return to class prepared instead of left behind by poverty.

School Construction

In Yayah Village, we work toward safer, closer, and more accessible learning spaces for children who travel too far for education.

School Renovation

We restore classrooms with roofing, painting, repairs, and improved learning conditions that children deserve.

Furniture Supply Programme

Desks and chairs reduce overcrowding and create a safer, more effective learning environment for pupils and teachers.

Impact & Story

Behind every intervention is a child waiting for a fair chance.

Children walk miles to school.

Some begin their day before sunrise and return home tired, hungry, and with limited time to study. A closer school changes what their future can become.

Girls miss school due to lack of hygiene materials.

For many girls, a single period can turn into missed classes and quiet shame. Our response is straightforward: provide practical menstrual health support so learning does not stop when dignity is protected.

This is how small, consistent interventions become long-term community change: by removing barriers that should never exist in the first place.
